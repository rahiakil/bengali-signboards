Seattle · painted boards · September 2026

Boards made to be read while walking.

These are contemporary painted panels in a Bengali folk hand: flat saffron grounds, black outline, repeating borders, figures that stay clear at a distance. They come from the UVE Decoration group. This page is a short exhibition so someone at the Chicago Department of Aviation, or the Department of Cultural Affairs and Special Events, can decide whether the work holds up.

What you are looking at

Six views are finished panels photographed straight on or standing. One is the larger river boards outdoors, so the scale is visible. Process photos, group portraits, and snapshots that do not show the boards clearly were left out.

Artist names and short biographies are not on this page yet. They will sit under each board when the makers send them. Until then, treat every panel as community work from UVE Decoration, not as a named commission.

How Chicago’s airports actually show art

O’Hare and Midway are owned and operated by the Chicago Department of Aviation. Together with the Department of Cultural Affairs and Special Events, the Department of Aviation keeps the City’s airport collection: permanent works by dozens of artists, and rotating exhibitions. The public pages are O’Hare Airport Art and Midway Airport Art.

A rotating exhibition

This is the nearer fit for these boards. A loaned set can hang for a defined season on an existing wall or in a display run, then come home. The City programs those shows. A link to this page is a viewing set, not an application, and it does not put anything in a terminal by itself.

A permanent fixture

Permanent pieces are commissioned. The current path is a Request for Qualifications, then a proposal only if the artist or team is shortlisted. In August 2026 the City issued an RFQ for seven integrated works at the new O’Hare Concourse D, about $3.8 million in total, with commissions in the range of $300,000 to $500,000. Those sites call for lighting, suspended and outdoor sculpture, and wall work built for the architecture. Submissions go through CaFÉ. The RFQ and its deadline are here: Concourse D RFQ.

These panels are graphic, durable in idea, and readable in a corridor. They are not yet a site-specific concourse installation. A rotating loan is the honest first ask. A permanent commission would mean a separate qualifications packet, dimensions, fabrication, and a team that can build for an airport.

What can be given for display

The makers can offer the City a written license to exhibit the physical boards and to photograph them for labels and press during the loan. That is a right to display, for a stated time and place. Copyright stays with the makers unless they later sign a separate assignment. Nothing on this page transfers copyright by itself.

Before any hanging, the note should name the works, the term, the walls, insurance, who may photograph them, and that the boards return at the end. Biographies and individual credits will be added to that note when they are provided.

How to proceed

  1. Share this page with anyone who needs to judge the boards in one sitting.
  2. For a rotating show, send the link to the Department of Aviation and the Department of Cultural Affairs and Special Events with a one-page loan offer: how many boards, approximate sizes, and the display license above.
  3. For a permanent O’Hare commission, use the Concourse D RFQ if it is still open, or the next public call. That packet asks for qualifications and work samples, not a finished folk panel dropped into a folder.
  4. Add names and short biographies here when they arrive, then refresh the page so the labels match the people who painted the boards.
